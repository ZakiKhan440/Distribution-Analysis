Background

Context & Objective

The distributor network in Islamabad serves thousands of outlets across the city and adjacent Rawalpindi. This analysis was conducted to evaluate whether the current single-warehouse model is sufficient, or whether a second warehouse is needed to serve outer zones more efficiently.

Two datasets were used: Firm (own company outlets) and Competitor (full market). Distances were calculated via two methods — Google Maps routing (realistic road distance) and straight-line (as-the-crow-flies) — then compared to ensure conclusions were robust regardless of methodology.

Key Findings

What the Data Tells Us

Five consistent signals emerged from both measurement methodologies, pointing to the same strategic conclusion.

54–62%
Volume concentrated within 15 km
Both datasets confirm that more than half of all Firm volume sits within 15km of the warehouse — strong evidence the current location is optimal.
< 3%
Volume beyond 30 km
Only 501 LPD (straight line) and 483 LPD (Google Maps) sit beyond 30km. Far too small to justify the fixed costs of a second warehouse.
20–30 km
Operational stretch zone
Firm share in this band is 19–23%, showing some opportunity but insufficient volume to justify a new warehouse. Route optimisation is the right lever.
15–20 km
Peak Firm contribution zone
Whether measured by road or straight-line, the highest Firm volume contribution sits within 15–20km. A single central warehouse captures this efficiently.
Consistent
Both methods agree
Google Maps routing (19,744 LPD) and straight-line (19,657 LPD) produce near-identical totals — the volume baseline and warehouse economics are unchanged.
63–67%
Competitor also clusters within 15 km
Even the full market (Competitor) data shows 63–67% of volume within 15km. Market behaviour is consistent — demand is genuinely close to the current location.

Conclusion

Strategic Recommendation

Analyst Recommendation
A second warehouse is not recommended. Both datasets independently confirm that demand clusters tightly within 20km of the current location, and outer zones hold insufficient volume to justify expansion.
The 20–30km stretch zone presents a route optimisation opportunity — not a warehousing one. Islamabad and Rawalpindi remain well-covered from a single, centrally-located distribution point.
